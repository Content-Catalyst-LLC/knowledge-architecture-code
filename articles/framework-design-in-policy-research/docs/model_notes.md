# Model Notes: Framework Design in Policy Research

## Core Idea

Framework design in policy research organizes policy problems, evidence, causal pathways, stakeholders, institutions, options, criteria, implementation pathways, evaluation logic, equity context, and governance records into a structure that can support public reasoning and institutional learning.

## Core Layers

1. **Problem layer**
   - problem definition
   - scope
   - affected population
   - jurisdiction
   - historical context
   - framing assumptions

2. **Evidence layer**
   - administrative data
   - evaluation evidence
   - survey data
   - qualitative research
   - legal authority
   - budget and fiscal data
   - community knowledge

3. **Causal layer**
   - mechanisms
   - theory of change
   - assumptions
   - risks
   - external conditions
   - expected outcomes

4. **Actor and institution layer**
   - stakeholders
   - affected communities
   - implementing agencies
   - legal institutions
   - service providers
   - funders
   - public accountability actors

5. **Option and decision layer**
   - policy options
   - decision criteria
   - option scores
   - trade-offs
   - feasibility
   - equity analysis
   - risk analysis

6. **Implementation and evaluation layer**
   - implementation pathway
   - indicators
   - baselines
   - evaluation questions
   - feedback loops
   - revision triggers

7. **Governance layer**
   - review status
   - source review
   - equity review
   - AI retrieval rules
   - revision history
   - institutional memory

## Key Review Questions

- Is the problem definition explicit?
- Is evidence linked to claims and assumptions?
- Are policy options connected to causal mechanisms?
- Are affected communities and institutions included?
- Are trade-offs and value judgments visible?
- Is equity analysis built into the framework?
- Is the framework evaluable?
- Can assumptions and recommendations be revised over time?
