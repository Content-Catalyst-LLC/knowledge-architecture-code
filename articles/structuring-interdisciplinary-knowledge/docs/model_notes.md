# Model Notes: Structuring Interdisciplinary Knowledge

## Core Idea

Structuring interdisciplinary knowledge means connecting fields without flattening their differences. It requires careful treatment of concepts, disciplines, methods, evidence systems, vocabularies, crosswalks, and governance.

## Core Layers

1. **Discipline layer**
   - fields
   - subfields
   - disciplinary scope notes
   - institutional context
   - evidence traditions

2. **Concept layer**
   - preferred labels
   - alternate labels
   - definitions
   - scope notes
   - disciplinary meaning
   - contested terms

3. **Method layer**
   - experimental methods
   - observational methods
   - modeling methods
   - legal interpretation
   - archival analysis
   - ethnography
   - conceptual argument

4. **Evidence layer**
   - datasets
   - models
   - primary sources
   - official records
   - legal authority
   - community knowledge
   - peer-reviewed literature

5. **Crosswalk layer**
   - exactMatch
   - closeMatch
   - broadMatch
   - narrowMatch
   - relatedMatch
   - contestedMatch
   - notEquivalent

6. **Governance layer**
   - crosswalk review
   - scope-note review
   - source diversity review
   - community knowledge review
   - AI retrieval review
   - revision tracking

## Key Review Questions

- Does each concept have a disciplinary context?
- Does each concept have a scope note?
- Does each relationship have a typed crosswalk relationship?
- Are vague "related" links minimized?
- Are methods and evidence types documented?
- Are contested or politically loaded terms flagged?
- Are community-governed forms of knowledge protected?
- Can AI retrieval distinguish similarity from equivalence?
