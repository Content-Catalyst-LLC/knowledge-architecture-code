# Model Notes: Knowledge Systems in Research Institutions

## Core Idea

A research institution becomes a knowledge system when its outputs are organized into durable structures that preserve context, relationships, provenance, governance, and responsible reuse.

## Institutional Knowledge-System Layers

1. **Research objects**
   - publications
   - datasets
   - software
   - laboratory records
   - protocols
   - grant records
   - community agreements
   - public reports
   - institutional archives

2. **Metadata and context**
   - creators
   - projects
   - methods
   - dates
   - access restrictions
   - provenance
   - status
   - repository location

3. **Repositories and preservation**
   - institutional repositories
   - data repositories
   - software archives
   - library records
   - archival collections
   - persistent identifiers

4. **Governance**
   - ethics protocols
   - data-management plans
   - access restrictions
   - sensitivity labels
   - community agreements
   - revision records
   - AI retrieval policies

5. **Semantic relationships**
   - usesDataset
   - generatedByCode
   - governedByProtocol
   - storedInRepository
   - translatesFindingsFrom
   - citesSource
   - fundedByGrant
   - createdByUnit

## Review Questions

- Are research objects findable?
- Do research objects carry enough metadata?
- Are relationships among publications, datasets, code, methods, protocols, and repositories traceable?
- Are access and reuse conditions documented?
- Are community-governed or sensitive knowledge objects protected?
- Are institutional records connected across units?
- Can AI-assisted retrieval distinguish public, restricted, provisional, and archival material?
